# Research: checking the cubical rules against Cubical Agda — 2026-10-25

The kernel's algorithm is proved sound in Agda, but whether the 404 rules of the
cubical theory describe cubical type theory is the largest open question in
this project ([TRUST.md](TRUST.md)). This note reports a first mechanical test
of that question. Every judgment of an accepted certificate is translated into
Cubical Agda, and Agda checks it independently of Horn's rules. A judgment that
Agda accepts holds in Agda's model of cubical type theory. A judgment that Agda
rejects points either at a rule or at a difference between the two theories,
and each one is classified below.

Nothing here changes what Horn trusts. The tools live in
[scripts/adequacy](../scripts/adequacy) and are untrusted. Their verdicts are
evidence about rule instances, not a proof about the rules.

## Method

`horn cubical eval --emit` writes a script's theory, goals and oracle. The
oracle is decoded to a compact certificate. Then:

1. **Expand** (`expand.py`). Each step is re-matched against its rule, which
   gives the ground judgment it derives.
2. **Syntactic facts** (`check_syntax.py`). Shifts, substitutions on terms,
   dimensions, faces and systems, context lookup, scoping, level arithmetic,
   face entailment, `cover`, `fforall`, `local` and `boundary` are checked
   against reference definitions written from scratch. These do not follow the
   rules. Faces are decided in the face lattice (DNF with clause inclusion).
   Interval equations are decided in the free de Morgan algebra by evaluation
   in its four-element generator.
3. **Typing and equality** (`to_agda.py`, `emit.py`, `run_agda.py`). The
   judgment `rhas d Γ ψ t A` becomes, for each DNF clause of `ψ`, an Agda
   definition `(i… : I) (x : Γ…) → A` with body `t`. The judgment
   `req d Γ ψ t u A` becomes `PathP (λ _ → A) t u` with body `λ _ → t`. Agda
   accepts the latter only if `t` and `u` are *definitionally* equal in Cubical
   Agda. The `def` constants become Agda definitions. Declared `data` types
   become Agda (higher) inductive types with pattern-matching eliminators.
   These are read from the generated rules: fields from `rhas.ctor/…`,
   endpoints from `req.ctor/…-0/1`. Up to K judgments per rule are checked,
   smallest first. Failing batches are bisected down to single judgments.

The translation maps `comp` to Agda's `primComp`, Glue to `primGlue` (with
Horn's Σ-encoded equivalences wrapped into Agda's `isEquiv` record), `bif`,
`natrec` and the circle to pattern-matching definitions, and systems to the
side whose face holds under the clause. β-redexes whose head Agda cannot infer
(`(λ x. b) a`, `fst (a, b)`, `(⟨i⟩ t) r`) are contracted before translation.
β holds in Agda, so this does not change what is tested. As a negative control,
the claim that `transport (ua not) true` is `true` is rejected by Agda with
`false != true`.

## The 99 rejections

Each rejection was traced to one of three causes.

### 1. HIT compositions assume regularity (genuine; 26 judgments)

All 26 rejections in `hit-params` come from two families of generated rules
for higher inductive types with parameters, or from structural rules applied
to their results.

- `req.comp-ty/X.ctor/X/c` reads a composition at the empty face as transport,
  constructor by constructor:
  `comp^i (ptrunc A) [ff ↦ _] (inc a) ≡ inc (comp^i A [ff ↦ _] a)`.
- `req.elim/X.hcomp` reads a composition along a constant line of the data
  type as the formal `hcomp`.

In Coquand, Huber and Mörtberg's semantics of HITs (*On Higher Inductive
Types in Cubical Type Theory*, LICS 2018, §2.4), a HIT has a transport `trans`
and a formal `hcomp`, and composition is *defined* as

```text
comp^i A [φ ↦ u] u0 := hcomp^i_{A(1)} [φ ↦ squeeze^i A 0F u] (trans^i A 0F u0)
```

At `φ = 0` this is `hcomp [] (trans u0)`, a formal element that is only
*propositionally* equal to `trans u0`. Along a constant line, `trans` is not
the identity on neutral elements. Horn's two rule families therefore hold only
if (R1) an empty `hcomp` is the identity and (R2) transport along constant HIT
lines is the identity. These are regularity properties that the CHM model does
not have, and Cubical Agda, which implements CHM, confirms both differences.
For example:

```text
Horn:  comp^i (ptrunc (ua not i)) [ff ↦ _] (inc true)  ≡  inc false
Agda:  that composition reduces to  hcomp [] (inc false),  which is stuck
```

Horn's constructor clause itself agrees with CHM. CHM's rule
`trans^i (Susp A) φ (merid a r) = merid (ctrans^i A φ a) r` is exactly the
right-hand side Horn uses. The difference is only that Horn has one `comp`
where CHM has `trans` and `hcomp`. The face can also *become* `ff` by
substitution, so Horn cannot be read as "`comp` at a literal `ff` means
`trans`" either. Translating such compositions as Agda's `transp`
(`HORN_COMP_FF=transp`) accepts the constructor cases with closed endpoints.
It fails on substituted instances, as expected.

This does not show that the theory is inconsistent. It shows that these rules
are not justified by the standard model of HITs. Making them so means either
separating `trans` and `hcomp` for data types as CHM does, or giving a model in
which (R1) and (R2) hold.

### 2. Horn follows CCHM; Agda implements CHM-style composition (69 judgments)

Horn states composition in Π, Σ, Glue and the universe as in Cohen, Coquand,
Huber and Mörtberg (CCHM), where `comp` is primitive and filling is
`comp [φ ↦ u, (i = 0) ↦ a] a`. Cubical Agda implements every composition as
`hcomp ∘ transp`, with fillers built from `transp`. The two agree up to paths
but not definitionally on neutral terms. Agda cannot even prove
`hcomp [] x ≡ x` definitionally for `x : ℕ`. These rejections are therefore
not evidence against Horn's rules, but Agda cannot confirm them:

| Rule | Rejections | Agda's stuck form |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `req.comp-ty/int` (and the same form for any closed data type) | 13 | empty `hcomp` on a neutral; CCHM's discrete types make it the identity |
| `req.comp-pi-app` | 10 | empty `hcomp` around `transp` on an abstract type |
| `req.comp-sigma` | 17 | CCHM filler vs Agda's `transpFill` |
| `req.comp-glue` | 15 | as above |
| `req.comp-universe` | 1 | as above |
| `rhas.glue` | 4 | Agda level metavariables (translator; fixed after the run, not yet rerun) |
| other (`glue-face`, …) in circle and poly | 9 | as above |

### 3. Resource limits (9 judgments)

Agda ran out of memory (6 GB per process) on nine of the largest Glue
judgments (`req.comp-glue`, `req.glue-face`, `rhas.glue`, `req.comp-sigma`).
They are undecided.

## What this does and does not show

- It checks **instances**, not rules. A rule whose sampled instances all
  pass may still have a false instance, and 42 used core rules have not been
  checked at all. These are mostly the context and system bookkeeping
  (`rctx.*`, `coherent.*`, `agrees.*`, `overlaps.*`), whose content reaches
  Agda only through the judgments they support. The `ff` cases
  (`rhas.false`, `req.false`) are vacuous.
- The validator itself is untrusted: the expansion, the reference syntax
  operations, the translation (system selection, β pre-contraction, the
  Glue wrapper) and Agda. An error there could hide a bad rule, though not
  make a false judgment pass unless the translation maps it to a true one.
- Agda accepting a judgment means it holds in Cubical Agda. Agda is not the
  CCHM model, which is why section 2 is unconfirmed rather than refuted.

## Next steps

1. Decide how HIT composition should be stated (section 1): add CHM-style
   `trans`/`hcomp` for data types, or keep the regular reading and look for a
   model that supports it.
2. Check rule schemata rather than instances: translate a rule with its
   metavariables as Agda variables and its premises as hypotheses. This would
   cover rules no script exercises.
3. Rerun circle and poly with the fixed Glue wrapper and more memory, and
   include `category.hcs`.

## Reproducing

Requires Python 3, a release build of Horn, and Agda 2.8.0
(`nix-shell -p agda`).

```sh
scripts/adequacy/run.sh /tmp/adequacy bool 20                       # one script
HORN_COMP_FF=transp scripts/adequacy/run.sh /tmp/adequacy hit-params 20
python3 scripts/adequacy/summary.py comp 20 /tmp/adequacy            # per-rule table
```

The logs of the run reported here are in
[scripts/adequacy/results](../scripts/adequacy/results).
